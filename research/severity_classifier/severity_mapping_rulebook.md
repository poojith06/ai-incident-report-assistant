# Rulebook: Severity Mapping Across Various Domains

This rulebook provides an exhaustive mapping of situations to severity levels based solely on the provided research papers. The mappings cover different domains, including patient safety, traffic crashes, occupational injuries, IT incidents, and construction accidents.

## 1. Primary Care Patient Safety Incidents
*Based on the "Primary Care Harm Severity Classification System, 2018" (Cooper et al., 2018)*

*   **No harm**: Any incident that ran to completion but no harm occurred to the patient.
*   **No harm outcome due to mitigating action**: Any incident that had the potential to cause harm to a patient but resulted in no harm.
*   **Mild harm**: An incident in which:
    *   (i) patient was harmed, with mild and short-term impact, on physical, mental or social functioning, that was expected to resolve in a few hours;
    *   (ii) patient was harmed but required no or minimal intervention/treatment, e.g. anti-emetic, oral antibiotic or repeat of a minor procedure such as vaccination or insertion of contraceptive implant; and/or
    *   (iii) patient or their loved ones experienced transient emotional distress but no long-term consequences and incident report contains words, e.g. angry, anxious, confused, distressed, frightened, frustrated, humiliated or upset, that might describe a feeling that occurs at the time of the incident but soon passes.
*   **Moderate harm**: An incident in which:
    *   (i) patient was harmed, causing a medium-term impact on physical, mental or social functioning that was expected to resolve in days;
    *   (ii) patient required medical intervention in the form of treatment, e.g. antibiotics or intravenous fluids;
    *   (iii) patient required short-term hospitalization for assessment and/or minor treatment in either ED or a hospital ward; and/or
    *   (iv) patient or their loved ones experienced psychological difficulty of a more longstanding nature but not requiring formal treatment, e.g. as indicated by evidence in the report of more longstanding anxiety, insomnia, or low mood.
*   **Severe harm**: An incident in which:
    *   (i) patient was harmed, causing a major long-term or permanent impact on physical, mental or social function or shortening of life-expectancy;
    *   (ii) patient was harmed and required major medical or surgical intervention that, most often, was delivered in a hospital setting, e.g. cardioversion, any major surgery;
    *   (iii) patient was harmed and required prolonged hospitalization or admission to CCU, HDU and/or ICU; and/or
    *   (iv) patient or their loved ones experienced enduring psychological difficulty that required specialist treatment, e.g. as indicated in the report by evidence of chronic anxiety or depression or psychosis.
*   **Death**: Incident in which, on the balance of probabilities, death of the patient was caused or brought forward in the short term by the incident.
*   **Insufficient detail**: Incident for which the report carries insufficient information to evaluate the severity of harm.

## 2. World Health Organization’s (WHO) International Classification for Patient Safety
*Based on descriptions of harm severity referenced in Cooper et al. (2018)*

*   **None**: Outcome was not symptomatic or no symptoms were detected and no treatment was required.
*   **Mild**: Patient outcome was symptomatic, symptoms were mild, loss of function or harm was either minimal or intermediate but short-term and no intervention or only a minimal intervention, e.g. extra observation, investigation, review or minor treatment, was required.
*   **Moderate**: Patient outcome was symptomatic, required more than a minimal intervention, e.g. additional operative procedure or additional therapeutic treatment, and/or an increased length of stay and/or caused permanent or long-term harm or loss of function.
*   **Severe**: Patient outcome was symptomatic, required a life-saving or other major medical/surgical intervention, shortened life expectancy and/or caused major permanent or long-term harm or loss of function.
*   **Death**: On balance of probabilities, death was caused or brought forward in the short-term by the incident.

## 3. Traffic Crash Severity (KABCO Scale)
*Based on Highway Safety Manual definitions referenced in Oliaee et al. (2023)*

*   **K**: Fatal injury
*   **A**: Severe injury
*   **B**: Moderate injury
*   **C**: Complaint
*   **O**: No injury

## 4. Occupational Injuries (OSHA Severe Injury Reports)
*Based on OSHA classification schemes referenced in Goldberg (2022) and Khairuddin et al. (2022/2024)*

In these datasets, severe workplace injuries are mapped based on two binary situations/outcomes:
*   **Hospitalization**: 
    *   Yes (Worker was admitted to a hospital)
    *   No
*   **Amputation**: 
    *   Yes (Worker suffered an amputation)
    *   No

## 5. Construction Collapse Accidents
*Based on China's 2021 production safety accident report and investigation regulations (Luo et al., 2023)*

Severity levels are classified based on the number of deaths, injuries, and economic losses into four categories:
*   **General accidents**
*   **Large accidents**
*   **Major accidents**
*   **Particularly significant accidents**

## 6. IT Incident Severity
*Based on the level of impact for each incident in AIOps (Ahmed et al., 2023)*

*   **1**: High
*   **2**: Medium
*   **3**: Low
*   **4**: None

## 7. Hospital-level Patient Safety Incidents (Severity Assessment Codes - SAC)
*Based on US Veterans Administration risk ratings referenced in Wang & Magrabi (2025)*

Risk ratings are assigned based on severity and recurrence likelihood:
*   **SAC1**: Extreme
*   **SAC2**: High
*   **SAC3**: Medium
*   **SAC4**: Low
